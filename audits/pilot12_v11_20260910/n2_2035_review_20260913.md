## Follow-up: amplitude contrast against the broader edge structure

The user identifies the distinguishing feature as the spikes standing roughly
4–5 times above the rest of the edge mode. A single-mode diagnostic confirms
that contrast for the strongest peak. Define the radial amplitude envelope
`B(r)=max_h |xi_h(r)|` and use its median over a broad outer interval as the
background. Taking the maximum over harmonics first avoids dilution by the
many weak or unused stored harmonic rows. The median includes the spike
samples here; no hand-selected spike removal is performed.

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 0.80–1.00 | 0.207239 | 4.825 |
| 0.85–1.00 | 0.216548 | 4.618 |
| 0.90–1.00 | 0.232183 | 4.307 |

With the 0.90–1.00 reference, the r=0.965 peak (amplitude 1) has contrast
4.307 and signed FWHM 1.163 intervals. The r=0.975 peak (amplitude 0.787882)
has contrast 3.393 and FWHM 1.446. Thus not every peak is individually 4–5
times the reference; the strongest one is, with a consistent estimate across
these reasonable outer intervals.

A candidate edge-spike branch could combine narrow signed width, high
peak/background contrast, and the existing appreciable-amplitude requirement.
Contrast could justify a less restrictive width cutoff for strongly enhanced
edge spikes while avoiding a blanket radial-cutoff change. This is a proposed
diagnostic direction only: background interval and rejection thresholds are
not calibrated or adopted. A weak outer tail must not reject solely because
its background is almost zero. Broad peaks also require the width condition.
No continuum extrapolation is needed for this morphology measurement.
