## Follow-up: effective noise length and harmonic-incoherence settings

Effective length is a radial participation measure. For eligible centers,
`h[m,i]=(xi[m,i+1]-2*xi[m,i]+xi[m,i-1])/4`,
`e[i]=dr*sum_m h[m,i]^2`, and
`L_eff=dr*(sum_i e[i])^2/sum_i e[i]^2` on the native uniform grid.
It equals `N_eff*dr`; it is not the distance between the first and last
nonzero/noisy samples. Each contiguous outside-gap region is considered
separately, with no pooling across crossings. All three samples of each
stencil must belong to the same region.

For N10/3470 the outside samples run from r=0.01 to 0.07. Eligible centers
run from 0.015 through 0.065, because the two boundary samples lack a
complete stencil in that region. The crossing is r=0.0747777; structure
at r>=0.075 is inside the gap and does not contribute to this diagnostic.
The leading shares of eligible high-pass energy are:

| Center r | Share |
| --- | ---: |
| 0.055 | 18.6342% |
| 0.060 | 39.8224% |
| 0.065 | 35.8976% |

These three centers carry 94.3542% of the eligible energy. The participation
calculation gives N_eff=3.082624 and L_eff=0.0154131. The cut L_eff>=0.04
requires at least eight effective equally weighted centers at nr=201.

The separate `BAD_INTERIOR_HARMONIC_INCOHERENCE` gate uses the product
`S=f_core * J_core * N_eff_core * (1-C_adj)` and rejects at strict `S>0.1`.
Its active v11 settings are core r<=0.5, adjacent-coherence harmonic activity
cut >=0.005 of core energy, maximum relative radial lag +/-5 grid intervals
(+/-0.025 at nr=201), and calibrated nr=201. These are full-core measurements,
not sliding-window or continuum-side measurements.

- `f_core` is core energy divided by total energy.
- `J_core` averages base-2 Jensen-Shannon divergence between the harmonic
  energy-fraction distributions at neighboring radial samples, weighted by
  `sqrt(W[i]*W[i+1])`. It uses all stored harmonics.
- `N_eff_core` averages `1/sum_m p[m,i]^2`, where
  `p[m,i]=xi[m,i]^2/W[i]`, weighted by W over the core.
- `C_adj` averages the maximum absolute normalized dot product of each pair
  of adjacent active stored harmonic rows, allowing the specified radial lag.
  Pair weights are the geometric mean of their core energies. Opposite signs
  can therefore be coherent. Only this coherence calculation uses the 0.5%
  harmonic activity cut. There is no independent C or N_eff rejection cutoff;
  the saved N_eff>3 fractions remain audit-only.

For N10/3470, `f_core=0.999134`, `J_core=0.0619673`,
`N_eff_core=2.39956`, `C_adj=0.923494`, hence `S=0.0113661<0.1`.
Active stored rows are 30–35, 58 and 67, but only pairs within 30–35 enter
C_adj. Rows 58/67 lack active immediate neighbors; they still contribute
to J_core and N_eff_core. Rows 32/33 contain 87.1035% of core energy, while
strict r<0.1 contains 5.35267%. The score is therefore dominated by the
coherent main structure and is insensitive to some weaker localized noise.

negligible noise. The radial-window/region selection and thresholds require
training-set calibration; removing dominant harmonic rows would also risk
discarding noisy portions of those same rows. No such change is adopted here.

## Proposed simultaneous-harmonic noise branch: exploratory pair check

User proposed a continuum-independent distributed-noise branch, combined by
OR with the existing continuum-side branch. At each native radius define
`q[i]=sum_m h[m,i]^2` and `N_hf[i]=q[i]^2/sum_m h[m,i]^4`, for the same signed
high-pass operator used above. If q=0, the point cannot qualify. This is
simultaneous participation; first summing over radius and then measuring
harmonic participation would count harmonics peaking at different radii.

For each window, keep only centers with N_hf>=T_N when accumulating HF energy
and radial participation. The global denominator is the original full-domain
mode/reference energy; the local denominator is all raw energy in that same
window. The HF numerator and length use exactly the same qualifying centers.
Keep all measurements for a witness in one window, never combine separate
windows' maxima. The diagnostic scan computes the high pass before applying
any mask and requires each three-point stencil to lie inside its window.
The window width is in normalized radius; no resampling is used.

Exploratory scans used N_hf cuts 3/4/5 and window widths 0.04/0.06/0.08/0.10,
with no calibrated new rejection thresholds. For each width/cut, the reported
example is the window with largest qualifying HF energy. Source fingerprints
still match the saved rules inputs. Recomputed unmasked HF and top-two
reference energy agree with the shared continuum-noise extractor.

The pointwise values support the proposed distinction in these examples:

- N10/3470: N_hf=6.78–14.09 at r=0.070–0.105; its raw-energy peak at r=0.130
  has N_hf=1.68. Strong main-structure HF at r=0.140 has N_hf=2.18.
- N5/2352: the crossing feature at r=0.160 has N_hf=1.89; the main peak at
  r=0.225 has N_hf=1.78.

The original 20% local-fraction cut would still miss the N10 example.
Thresholds for the additional branch require training calibration; these two
cases demonstrate diagnostic separation, not a validated gate. Retaining the

candidate way to respect the earlier concern about edge-mode dilution.

N_hf measures multiplicity, not statistical independence: coherent rough
structure can also have high participation. The energy and span conditions
remain necessary. This proposed rejection-only OR branch cannot rescue
N5/2352 from its existing crossing-window rejection. No production branch,
gate configuration or label has been changed.
